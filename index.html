Executive summary

Three findings that matter

A concise interpretation before the detailed statistical evidence.

Remote roles report higher pay

After 3×IQR trimming, remote respondents earn about $14.1k more on average and $12.9k more at the median than hybrid respondents. The gap persists across robust analyses.

Degree groups differ—unevenly

US/Canada Bachelor's respondents earn about $16.2k less on average than either Master's or Professional degree holders; the latter two groups have almost identical means.

Extreme values matter

Salary distributions have a long upper tail. Removing 1.46% of eligible work-mode records under 3×IQR shifts the estimated salary gap, so filtering choices should be disclosed.

Interpret the controls

Sample size changes uncertainty; replications mainly change Monte Carlo precision; confidence level changes the span of the interval. Neither setting changes the observed group means used in the model.

Exploratory, not causal

Dataset and analysis populations

The analysis uses the cleaned 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey supplied for MIE1624. The initial EDA sample contains 20,399 rows after deduplication. Work-arrangement analyses include Hybrid and Remote respondents internationally. Education analyses are restricted to Bachelor's, Master's and Professional degree respondents in the US and Canada.

Education-group sample sizes before 3×IQR are 2,978, 1,084 and 301, respectively; after trimming they are 2,917, 1,055 and 287.

Data quality and outlier treatment

Exact duplicate rows and invalid annual salary values were excluded for group comparisons, without filtering on unrelated questionnaire fields. There was no arbitrary minimum salary cutoff. Work-mode analyses compared 1.5×IQR and 3×IQR rules using the same threshold for Hybrid and Remote. The selected 3×IQR rule removes 1.46% of eligible work-mode records. A separate common 3×IQR threshold was computed for the eligible North American education subsample, removing 2.38%.

These filters trim extremely high reported salaries but do not prove those observations were erroneous. Conclusions refer to the retained subsets.

Inferential methods and bootstrap interpretation

Q2 includes manually calculated pooled and built-in t-tests, the Levene equal-variance check and Welch's t-test. Q3 uses one-way and Welch ANOVA. Each main bootstrap analysis runs 10,000 group-wise resamples with replacement at original group sizes. Bootstrap p-values in Q2(d), Q2(f) and Q3(d) use null-centered reference distributions, with +1 correction (minimum nonzero p ≈ 0.0001).

Standardized figures present mean differences divided by fixed standard errors, keeping the observed group separation visible. Confidence intervals include Welch's t-based and percentile-bootstrap estimates. Results must not be interpreted as exact probabilities that a null hypothesis is true.

Limitations and presentation choices

Survey participation is not a probability sample of the developer population. Country, job title, experience, hours worked, employer characteristics and other confounders were not jointly controlled. Small groups may have unstable means; the Q1 age field includes implausible values, and salary distributions contain extremely long upper tails.

Some figures are static by design because the notebook provides image outputs but not the respondent-level data or histogram-bin aggregates needed to reconstruct fully interactive charts without inventing underlying observations. The salary comparisons and filters on this dashboard are interactive, while source plots remain exact.
